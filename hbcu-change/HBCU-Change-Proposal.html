A proposal for our next stage at HBCU Change

As we bring more schools on board, let’s make the work easier to manage.

I’ve put together a proposal to move our websites and more of the work around them into GoHighLevel. We could give each school more freedom in its design, automate routine follow-ups, and make progress easier for the team to review.

See what I’m proposing See the costs

The apps would remain at the centre. This proposal is about how we promote them and manage the work around them.

What I’ve noticed while working on the websites

We’ve built a starting point. Now we can make it easier to build on.

We’ve brought four schools on board over eight months. Each partnership has added to our experience of presenting a school, promoting its app, and supporting its campaigns.

While building the pages, I’ve also seen where the current setup makes the work harder. Each school has its own fonts and colours, but its pages sit within HBCU Change’s shared website styling. Getting the right look has often meant custom code or graphics created separately in Canva.

There’s also a question worth preparing for: if two or three schools are getting ready to launch at the same time, how do we keep track of what each one needs and what the team should do next?

What I’m proposing

I’m proposing GoHighLevel as a place to manage our school websites, partnership follow-ups, campaign communication, and reporting. It brings those tools into one platform, so we could connect a form submission to the next action instead of treating each part as a separate task.

I would set up those connections around how the team works. Here’s what that could look like.

First, the school websites

Each school should look like itself. Right down to the fonts.

We already create pages around each school’s identity. The difficulty has been adapting them within a site whose main styling is set for HBCU Change.

With a separate website for each school in HighLevel, I could set its fonts, colours, and layouts directly. The team could still manage the websites from the same account, with less reliance on the workarounds I use now.

If a campaign needs a separate website, as FAMU’s did, we could also build it within the platform subscription instead of taking out another website plan. Domains and the work to build it would still have their own costs.

More control over each school’s design, with fewer workarounds when we need to make changes.

Next, the work behind each partnership

A school gets in touch. What happens next?

A representative fills out an inquiry form. They need a response, someone on the team needs to take the conversation forward, and a meeting may need to be booked.

I would set up a shared record for that conversation, with the school’s details, the person responsible, and the next action. As the partnership moves forward, the record would move through stages such as meeting booked, agreement in progress, assets needed, and ready to launch.

That’s what a pipeline means here: a board showing where each school stands, so the team can see what needs attention when several partnerships are moving at once.

Once a campaign is ready

Make it easy to find the app. Keep interested supporters informed.

A supporter might hear about a campaign through a social post, an email, or an event. The campaign page should explain what it is about and take them straight to the right school’s app.

If they choose to receive updates, we could also send information about that school or campaign. For example, someone who asks for FAMU campaign updates could receive the app links and a reminder before the campaign closes.

  • Prepare the promotion together. Plan posts for our connected social accounts around the campaign dates, so the team can review what is scheduled.
  • Send the right people the right updates. Use the school and campaign interests people select to decide which emails they receive.
  • Collect the campaign details once. Use a form for the campaign brief, dates, assets, and approvals, then use a short survey to gather feedback afterward.

Seeing what is working

Which schools need attention? Which campaigns are getting a response?

These are the questions I would build the reporting around. The team could use it to decide what to work on next, and the founders could review progress as more schools and campaigns come on board.

What does each school still need before launch?

See whether a school is waiting for a meeting, brand materials, content review, or launch approval. Alongside that, see who is handling the next action and how long the school has been waiting.

Before we commit to another launch date, we would have a clearer view of the work already in progress.

Did that interest lead to activity in the app?

A click tells us someone opened the app-store page. It does not tell us whether they installed the app, registered, or donated.

Those answers would need to come from the app’s own records. Once we know what data is available, we can decide whether and how to include it in the reports.

The next part of the picture

App registrations and donations would need app data. Connecting them to a particular campaign would require a reliable way to match that activity.

Depends on the app’s reporting options

We can measure recorded app-store clicks on the website, but they should never be reported as downloads or donations.

What it would cost

A similar monthly subscription. More of the tools we need.

We currently spend $95 a month across Squarespace, Mailchimp, and Notion. The proposed HighLevel plan is $97 a month before usage charges and extras.

The reason to consider it is what we could do with that subscription: manage the school websites, keep partnership details together, organise follow-ups, offer booking links, send campaign emails, collect assets, and plan social posts.

My work would be to set those tools up around our process, so the team has something useful to work with.

If we decide to go ahead

We can make the move in stages.

I would start with one school’s agreed setup, test how the pieces work together, and use what we learn before moving the others across.

The existing sites would stay available while their replacements are being prepared and checked.

    Agree on what we need first

    Decide which websites, follow-ups, and reports matter most. Confirm responsibilities, the setup fee, and a realistic schedule before the build starts.

    Apply the tested approach to the remaining schools, make their branding and campaign adjustments, and document the steps the team needs to follow.

Questions worth answering

A few things we should be clear about

Does this change the apps?

The apps would remain our fundraising product. This proposal covers the websites and the work around school partnerships and campaigns. Any connection to app data would be a separate part of the scope.

Do we need to move platforms to track app-store clicks?

No. We can add that tracking to Squarespace too. The reason to consider HighLevel is the wider combination of school websites, follow-ups, booking, and campaign tools. Click tracking is one part of the proposal.

Will the reports show every click and donation?

No. Browser settings and consent choices can affect website tracking, and repeated clicks are not necessarily different people. Donations need to come from the app or payment records. I would make those distinctions clear in the reports.

Can we manage the schools in one account?

That is the proposed approach: the HBCU Change team would manage the school websites and related work centrally. If school staff later need their own access, we would review how to separate their information and permissions.

Would we keep Notion?

Possibly. We would first check what the team uses it for and whether those needs are covered. Keeping it would put the proposed base subscription total at $107 a month.

Would everything happen automatically?

No. I would automate agreed routine steps, such as acknowledging an inquiry or sending a reminder. The team would still handle relationships, review assets and content, and approve launches.

Would there be costs while we move?

There may be a period when both platforms are active while replacement sites are built and tested. I would account for that in the schedule and budget, along with the setup fee and expected usage charges.

Let’s decide what would help us most.

I’m bringing this forward because I can see a way to make the websites easier to build and more of the work around them easier to manage.

If the direction makes sense, the next step is to choose our priorities. I can then put together a clear scope, cost, and rollout plan for us to review.
